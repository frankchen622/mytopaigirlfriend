## What is Hinge?

Hinge brands itself as "the dating app designed to be deleted"—a not-so-subtle dig at competitors that profit from keeping you single and swiping. Launched in 2012 and relaunched in 2016 with a relationship-focused redesign, Hinge emphasizes detailed profiles, conversation prompts, and meaningful connections over endless swiping.

Unlike Tinder's photo-first approach, Hinge profiles include multiple photos plus responses to prompts like "My most irrational fear," "I'm looking for," or "A life goal of mine." This provides more conversation fodder and helps users find compatible matches based on personality, not just appearance.

In 2026, Hinge has emerged as the go-to app for singles seriously seeking relationships. While other platforms accommodate both casual and serious dating, Hinge's branding, features, and user base lean heavily toward commitment-minded users. If you're looking for a hookup, Tinder or Bumble are better bets. If you want to find a partner to actually date (and potentially build a life with), Hinge is purpose-built for that goal.

Reddit's dating communities consistently rate Hinge highly for relationship-seeking, though with caveats: it works best in cities, requires patience, and the smaller user base (compared to Tinder) means fewer total matches. The consensus: if you're serious about finding a partner, Hinge is worth using alongside or instead of broader dating apps.

## Key Features

### 1. Profile Prompts Instead of Bios
Rather than a blank text box for a bio, Hinge provides dozens of prompts to respond to: "Two truths and a lie," "I know the best spot in town for," "My simple pleasures," etc. You choose three prompts and write responses.

This approach has two advantages: (1) eliminates the "I don't know what to write" paralysis that leads to empty bios, and (2) provides natural conversation starters. Instead of awkward "hey," you can comment on someone's answer to their prompt.

Reddit users overwhelmingly prefer this system to traditional bios. However, creativity matters—generic answers ("I love to travel," "Looking for someone who makes me laugh") still populate many profiles and provide little actual insight.

### 2. Like and Comment System
Instead of binary swiping, you "like" specific photos or prompt responses and optionally add a comment. This creates more meaningful engagement than Tinder's batch swiping. If they like you back (or you've already matched), conversation begins.

Free users get 8 likes per day. Premium (Hinge+) users get unlimited likes. This limitation frustrates some users but also prevents the mindless swiping that characterizes other apps. Hinge wants you to be selective.

### 3. Most Compatible
Daily algorithmic suggestion of someone Hinge thinks you'd match well with based on profile information and app usage patterns. Users report mixed results—sometimes spot-on, sometimes baffling. The feature at least introduces variety beyond your self-selected filtering.

### 4. Standout Feed
A curated selection of profiles Hinge thinks you'll find particularly attractive or compatible. You can send Roses (Hinge's version of Super Likes) to Standout profiles to get noticed. Free users get one Rose per week; premium users can buy more.

Hinge's free version is notably more generous than Tinder's—you can see who liked you without paying, which is a huge advantage. However, the 8 likes per day can feel restrictive, especially in cities with many potential matches. Most active users eventually upgrade to Hinge+ for unlimited likes and filters.

## Does "Designed to Be Deleted" Actually Mean Something?

Hinge's tagline is clever marketing, but does the company genuinely want you to find a partner and leave? The cynical view: no company wants to lose users. The optimistic view: creating successful matches builds brand loyalty and word-of-mouth marketing.

The truth is probably in between. Hinge's features do seem designed for meaningful connections—detailed profiles, conversation starters, relationship-focused prompts. But the monetization model still depends on active users, creating inherent tension between the stated mission and business incentives.

What matters for users: does Hinge work better than alternatives for finding relationships? Reddit's consensus is yes, with caveats about user base size and the need for patience. Whether Hinge "wants" you to succeed is less important than whether the platform's design helps you succeed—and evidence suggests it does.
